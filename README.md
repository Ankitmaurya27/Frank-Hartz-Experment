### About the Experiment 

The Franck-Hertz experiment (1914) gave direct evidence that atoms have
discrete energy levels. Electrons are emitted from a heated cathode and
accelerated towards a grid inside a tube filled with a gas. A small
retarding voltage between the grid and the collecting plate lets only
electrons with enough energy reach the plate.

As the accelerating voltage increases, the plate current rises. When the
electrons gain enough energy to excite the gas atoms (inelastic collision),
they lose that energy and the current drops sharply. This gives a series of
peaks and dips, and the voltage spacing between successive dips equals the
first excitation energy of the gas.

In this experiment, the setup is repeated with different inert gases
(for example neon and argon) to compare their excitation energies and
verify that each gas has its own characteristic energy levels.

**Learning objectives**
- Observe the current vs. accelerating voltage curve
- Measure the excitation energy from the spacing of the dips
- Compare the results for different inert gases
